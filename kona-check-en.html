How is the qualifying time calculated?

Where do the numbers come from?

The basis is the public IRONMAN results of the last few years per race plus the official IRONMAN qualification method from 2026: each age group gets one automatic World Championship slot for its winner, the remaining slots go by normalized performance (age-grading against that age group’s „Kona Standard“) to the fastest times in the field. The qualifying time shown is the slowest time that would typically still have earned a slot.

Is this a guarantee?

No. The actual cutoff time depends on the field strength on race day, the number of slots and roll-down (unclaimed slots pass down the ranking). Treat the number as a realistic guide, not a fixed limit. The value is the median across several editions; the range shows how much it varies from year to year.

Where is the World Championship held?

From 2026 the IRONMAN World Championship for men and women takes place together again in Kailua-Kona, Hawaii. The Kona/Nice split (2023–2025) is history – all races listed here qualify for Kona.

What is roll-down?

Not every qualified athlete accepts their World Championship slot. Unclaimed slots then roll down within the age group to the next-fastest athlete – that is roll-down. It is why a slightly slower time than the raw qualifying time often still makes it to Kona.

Do I qualify for Kona 2026 or 2027?

The qualifying window for the 2026 World Championship (Kona, 10 Oct 2026) closes in mid-August 2026. Races before that feed Kona 2026, later races feed the 2027 edition – also held in Kona. Only 2026 is officially confirmed so far.
